Cover form, not every botanical trait

Annuals, perennials, bulbs and leafy vegetables share Herb. Reeds and sedges share Grass. Rosette covers aloe, agave and similar radial plants. Epiphytic and aquatic plants use the form that fits them; those traits do not multiply the picker.

Every shape earns its place

Round crown, stepped triangle, spreading fan, low mound, diagonal leaf, upright tuft, paired canes, feather, curl, horizontal runner, pointed rosette and branched cactus. Different structures, not twelve kinds of leaf.

Small marks come first

Bold silhouettes carry the identity. Fine cuts disappear at small sizes. The same outline is used in the picker and canvas, with theme-aware edges and the original species colors. The neutral dot remains the fallback.

Catalog coverage and limits of this study

The local prepared catalog contains 95,972 Herbaceous, 29,712 Shrub, 24,604 Tree and 14,282 Climber records; 10,903 lack a habit. More specific growth-form data is uneven: 20,935 Epiphyte records, but only 2,803 Graminoid and 22 Fern, despite 11,483 Poaceae and 1,172 Polypodiaceae records. A symbol family should not simply mirror those fields. Shape suggestions need reviewed mappings and a manual override.

This preview uses catalog habit for the reference species, with explicit visual assignments for a few spreading plants, the fern and missing-habit Russian sage. These are study assignments, not saved classifications. Other files may fall back to a neutral dot where this small catalog excerpt has no record.
