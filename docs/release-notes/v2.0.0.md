# Canopi 2.0.0 Release Notes

## The map is the canvas

- Every Design is drawn directly on the map. Basemap, satellite imagery, LiDAR,
  contours and hillshade are the background of the design surface; there is no
  separate Location step or placement anchor.
- Every design object is geolocated: plants, zones, annotations and measurement
  guides are stored in WGS84 longitude/latitude. Panning, zooming and place
  search move only the view, never your objects.
- A new Design opens "Where is your site?" over a country-level view of where
  you last worked (or the world), not over the previous Design's site, and
  flies to the place you pick.
- Fit to Design, "Zoom to them" and Return to Design frame into the part of
  the map the title bar, rails and open panel leave visible, and status chips
  centre there.
- Map labels, text notes, measurements and plant labels stay readable in the
  dark theme: their colour follows the map underneath (basemap, satellite or
  no background) instead of the app theme, with a contrasting halo.
- The map credits stay readable as a pill beside the zoom controls; they fold
  into an (i) button when there is no room for them on one line (a narrow
  window, or a panel open in a narrow window) instead of wrapping under the
  panel.
- A panel's highlight on the map (a Calendar, Budget or Consortium row you
  point at or select) takes the new colours as soon as you switch theme,
  instead of keeping the old ones until it is drawn again.
- A map layer that cannot be drawn (a panel highlight, a LiDAR item, contours
  or hillshade) is left out with a quiet "A map layer couldn't be shown"
  notice; the map and your Design stay editable. "Map unavailable" is kept
  for failures of the map itself.

## Map layers

- Basemap: OpenFreeMap vector styles (Liberty, Positron, Bright, Dark) with
  labels in your language.
- Satellite: Google imagery, keyless by default; add your own Google Maps API
  key in the Satellite row to use the official Map Tiles API. Turning Satellite
  on hides the Basemap. Your Google key stays on this device and never
  enters Designs, exports or logs.
- Contours and hillshade stay available as map layers. Map layers are app
  settings shared by every Design.

## Site data, library and analysis

- Layers is one list in three sections: Design, Site data and Background.
  Site data shows this Design's terrain and height data with the results
  calculated from them nested underneath ("from Terrain · degrees"), and
  Contour lines and Hillshading say they come from online elevation, not
  from your data.
- "Add data" beside Site data is the one entry point: terrain or height
  from files, an item from your library, or the Data library. Importing
  from Layers adds the data to this Design when it is ready; its progress
  and Cancel show under Site data while you keep working.
- The Import dialog says which files Canopi accepts (single-band GeoTIFF,
  such as IGN LiDAR HD MNT, MNS or MNH tiles), lets you reorder or drop
  files, and refuses a name your library already uses with a suggestion.
- Select a site data row for its legend, opacity, Fit, Read values,
  Analyze… and Details, and to move it with Alt+↑/↓. Details show where a
  result comes from, whether it is out of date (with Refresh), its
  processing history and Run again with changes.
- The Data library and Analyze are dialogs over the map instead of a
  separate panel. Remove from Design never deletes library data; Delete
  everywhere, in the library, does.
- On the Web, Site data says that terrain and height data need Canopi
  Desktop and stay in the Design.

## Place search

- The place field in the title bar (or Ctrl+K) searches a place name or typed
  coordinates. Names are looked up through OpenStreetMap Nominatim on Enter
  only; coordinates go straight to the spot without a request. The field says
  "Press Enter to search" while a name waits to be searched.

## Workspace and panels

- Menus, submenus and right-click menus fit the window: a long menu scrolls
  inside, and the keyboard keeps the focused item in view.
- In a narrow window with a panel open, the tool rail keeps to icons (with
  named tooltips) so the map stays usable, and rulers start beside the rail.
- In a short window, the panel rail folds its last panels into a More menu
  instead of running down over the zoom controls, and the tool rail folds its
  last tools into More tools so Undo and Redo always stay in view. When a
  tool name would be cut off (long German names), the rail shows icons with
  named tooltips instead.
- The panel resize grip is quiet until you hover or focus it.
- Dialogs are modal: while one is open, the menus, title bar, rails, panels
  and shortcuts wait under it instead of opening menus over it.
- While "Where is your site?" shows, the open panel steps aside.
- The Start screen says where your Designs will appear and offers New Design
  when there are none yet. Recent Designs no longer show "0 plants".
- The plant database warning, and on Web the shell notice (such as a GeoJSON
  import error), take their own row below the title bar instead of covering
  controls or the open panel, and the map no longer shows through them.
- Plant counts, catalog results and panel counts are formatted for your
  language (175,473; 175 473 in French). Panel headers no longer show an
  unexplained number beside the title.
- Budget rows keep species names readable in the 440 px panel.
- The Data Library's type filter and import measurement use Canopi's own
  dropdown.

## Finding plants

- Every plant list (Plants in this Design, Budget, Consortium, Calendar targets,
  Favorites) has the same finder: press Ctrl+F in the open panel. It matches
  common names in every language the catalog knows, scientific names and
  codes, ignores accents and capitals, tolerates small typos and says what it
  searched instead.
- "Selected on map" narrows any of these lists to the species you selected on
  the map, and follows the selection as it changes.
- In Plants in this Design, matches are ringed on the map with "Zoom to them"
  and "Select all"; species not in the Design lead to the catalog. The rings
  keep a readable size when the whole Design is in view.
- A species a panel points at or edits (such as in Consortium) is
  ringed on the map in the same ochre highlight as finder matches, instead of
  a large teal disc.

## Plant catalog

- The Plant catalog has a title and a close button like every other panel.
- The command palette and menus call it Plant catalog, as the panel does.
  When its database file is missing or damaged, the notice says so in plain
  words: plant search and details are off, your Designs are safe, and
  reinstalling Canopi restores it.
- It opens on useful species: rated edible and multi-use plants first, then
  species with a name in your language, instead of obscure species in
  alphabetical order. Sort by Name, Height or Edibility instead (Web: Name).
- Quick filters (Edible, Nitrogen fixer, Trees, Shrubs) sit under the search;
  every other filter waits behind "Filters", and active filters show as tokens
  you can remove one by one or clear together.
- Rows show the common name over the scientific name, the plant's form,
  height, hardiness and edibility, its code when it is already in your Design,
  Place and a favourite star.
- Search uses the same field as other plant lists (Ctrl+F), marks the matches
  and says what it searched; empty results say why.
- Species details open with the plant's symbol, its name in your language over
  the scientific name and family, and its code when it is in your Design. A
  plant with no name in your language shows its English name marked "(en)".
- Key facts come first: form, stratum, height and width, hardiness with its
  USDA zones and lowest temperature, light, soil, moisture, growth, succession
  and the edible, medicinal and other-uses ratings. Anything the catalog lacks
  says "Not recorded". The longer sections open on demand.
- Photos move with arrows, dots or the arrow keys, and each one names its
  source and license, or says they are not recorded.
- When the species is in your Design, the details say how many plants it has,
  with Select them and Zoom to them; Place is always at hand.

## Saved views

- View › Save current view… keeps where the map looks, with a name and an
  optional title, inside the Design. View › Saved views goes back to one: the
  map flies there (or jumps when your system asks for reduced motion), and
  nothing in the Design moves or changes.
- View › Manage views… renames and deletes views. A delete can be undone, and
  Canopi asks first when a story uses the view.

## Menus

- File › Close Design (Ctrl+W on the desktop) saves any last change and returns
  to the Start screen. The Design stays in its file or Draft.
- On the desktop, File › Add data… imports terrain or height data into the
  open Design and File › Data library… opens the library. File › Export ›
  Budget as CSV… exports the Budget from the menu too.
- Edit › Deselect (Esc) and Edit › Unlock all, which unlocks every locked
  object in the Design as one Undo.
- Help › Getting started shows a short guide to the first steps.

## Right-click menu

- The floating icon bar over a selection is gone. Right-click an object, or
  press the Menu key or Shift+F10 with the map focused, for one menu in plain
  words with shortcuts: Cut, Copy, Paste, Duplicate, Select all of this
  species, Plant color, Plant symbol, Show or hide name, Bring to front, Send
  to back, Group, Ungroup, Save as stamp, Lock, Unlock and Delete. Commands
  that do not apply stay visible but disabled.
- Plant color and Plant symbol moved from the tool rail into this menu.
- Right-click the empty map to Paste at that spot or Select all.
- Lock is now Ctrl+Shift+L (Cmd+Shift+L on macOS) in the menus, the
  right-click menu and Keyboard shortcuts; Ctrl+L belongs to the browser's
  address bar on the Web.
- Rotation stays on the map: drag the handle above a rotatable selection.
- Rotate… (Edit menu, right-click menu or Ctrl+Alt+R) turns the selection by
  an angle you type, or step by 15°, about its center, as one Undo. Locked
  objects never turn.
- The rotation handle stays in the visible part of the map: a selection near
  the tool rail or under the title bar keeps its handle where you can reach it.
- A chip at the bottom of the map names what is selected ("Apricot",
  "12 plants · 3 species · 0.52 m apart", "Zone · Z04 · 118 m² · 46 m") with
  Select all of this species and Clear selection. It gives the spacing of
  selected plants, a zone's area and perimeter and a measurement's length,
  and names a zone you have not named by its type ("Rectangle zone"), never
  by an internal id.

## Calendar

- The action editor has the same header as every panel: Back to the list,
  the title and Close. Range, One day and Unscheduled are one segmented choice
  that shows which is selected; Completed is a switch; the description has
  room for notes on several lines.
- A Design without actions says "No actions yet" with Add action. "No actions
  match these filters" and Clear filters appear only when your own filters
  hide actions.
- The week starts on your language's first day (Monday in French, German,
  Spanish, Italian, Dutch, Russian and Chinese; Sunday in English,
  Portuguese, Japanese and Korean).
- Zone targets name a zone you have not named by its type and size
  ("Rectangle zone · 120 m²"), in the zone list and on the action, never by
  an internal id.

## Design tools

- Every drawing and placing tool shows a card beside the tool rail: the
  tool's name, what to do now (naming the species or stamp you are placing),
  and its keys, ending with what Esc does. Screen readers announce the tool
  you pick. Place plants offers Change species; Place a stamp counts the
  plants and species it places.
- The tool card starts with the species' symbol in its map colour (or the
  stamp icon) and puts the species or stamp name on its own line above what
  to do. The Place plants chooser shows each species with its symbol and
  colour, and rings its plants on the map only while you point at or focus
  its row, so nothing stays ringed while you place.
- Place plants works before you pick a species: its card offers a short
  chooser with the species already in the Design first, then your Favorites
  and recent picks, a search that finds plants the same way as every plant
  list, and a link to the full catalog. Clicking the map before choosing
  places nothing and points you to the chooser.
- While placing plants, the map previews the plant under the pointer: its
  symbol, a dashed ring for its mature width when the catalog knows it, and
  the distance to the nearest plant ("1.8 m to Apple").
- Plant a row uses the same tool card as every other tool: its Interval field
  and the live count of plants sit in the card, and Enter, Esc and Tab work
  there as before.
- Shift keeps a polygon zone's next edge on 45° angles, as it already did for
  Plant a row.
- The map is a keyboard stop: Tab to it (a blue ring shows it has focus), then
  use the tool keys, the Menu key or Shift+F10 for the right-click menu, and
  Esc. Screen readers announce it as "Design map" with its keys.
- Arrow keys nudge the selected objects on the map by 10 cm, or 1 m with
  Shift; a run of presses is one Undo, and locked objects stay put. F6 and
  Shift F6 move between the title bar, the tools, the map and the open panel.
  Help › Keyboard shortcuts lists both.
- Esc works as one chain on the map: it cancels what you are drawing or
  typing, then returns to Select, then clears the selection. After a text
  note is finished or cancelled, focus stays on the map so the next Esc
  still works.
- The text note field is named "Text note" for screen readers and prompts
  "Type the note".

## GeoJSON

- Export a Design's objects as a GeoJSON FeatureCollection, and import GeoJSON
  points, polygons and lines as plants, annotations, zones and measurement
  guides in one undoable step. Available on Desktop and Web.

## Saving

- Canopi now saves automatically, a moment after each change and whenever you
  switch away or close. New Designs are drafts, listed on the welcome screen,
  until you Save As a file. There are no more "unsaved changes" prompts: you
  are asked only if a save fails or the file was changed outside Canopi, and
  "Revert to version when opened" undoes a session's changes.

## Plant symbols

- 29 redrawn single-colour symbols in three families: plant form (the canopy
  tree is now a side view like the other forms), what it gives (fruit, nut,
  berries, grapes, flowers, nitrogen fixer) and what it does (vegetable, grain,
  spice, medicinal, pollinator, biomass, timber, fodder, windbreak, soil
  builder, fungi). The four abstract marks stay.
- Any symbol takes any colour. Detail is cut out and takes the outline colour,
  so symbols stay legible at small sizes and in dense plantings, and look the
  same on the map, in panels and in the PDF.
- The symbol picker groups the symbols by family, five to a row.

## Display on the map

- Plants in this Design › Display on the map colours plants by species (your
  colours), by stratum or in one colour. Stratum colours come from the
  stratum each species has in Consortium, with a legend and a grey for "No
  stratum yet"; the four colours stay apart for colour-blind readers. Your
  species colours are kept and come back when you switch to Species.
- Symbol size (50 to 200 %), Outline on or off and Labels (None, Codes,
  Names) sit in the same section. These choices are saved with the Design and
  the PDF's "As in the Design" colours follow them.
- View › Labels (or N on the map) cycles None, Codes and Names; a chip says
  how many plants in view carry one ("Codes shown for 70 of 282 plants in
  view").
- Soften background dims the map or satellite under your plants so small
  symbols stand out on busy imagery. It is kept on this device for every
  Design.

## Planting plan PDF

- File › Export to PDF opens one side sheet beside the live page preview:
  paper, what to include (plants, zones, notes, measurement guides, north
  arrow and scale), plant colours, field sheets, page thumbnails and Save.
  Zoom, orientation, split sheets and their review now sit in the same sheet
  for the selected page; drag the page or use the arrow keys to move it.
- Plant colours print As in the Design, in Grayscale (each colour gets its own
  grey, the same on every page) or in Black. The Design itself never changes.
- Map pages carry a north arrow beside the scale bar; one switch turns both
  off.
- The plant key is grouped by habit: Tree, Shrub, Herbaceous, Climber, then
  Other, with "(continued)" headings when a group runs into the next column.
  Names wrap instead of being cut.
- Names missing in your language show the English name marked "(en)",
  explained in the key header.
- Page 1 lists the plant symbols it uses.

## Intentional break with 1.x

- Canopi 2.0 reads and writes `.canopi` format version 8 only. Designs saved by
  Canopi 1.x (format 6 and older) and by 2.0 pre-releases (format 7) are
  refused with a clear message; there is no converter.
- LiDAR libraries created by earlier builds are deleted on first start and must
  be imported again.
- Older user data that Canopi 2.0 cannot read is set aside rather than migrated.
  Web Edition storage from 1.x is ignored.
- The Canvas2D fallback renderer is gone. If WebGL2 or the map cannot start, the
  canvas shows "Map unavailable" and the open Design stays loaded and saveable.

## Known limits

- Performance tuning for very dense Designs is scheduled after 2.0.
- Canvas PDF prints plans on white paper, without map backgrounds.
- On Web, the PDF key is one ungrouped list until the Web catalog carries plant
  habits.
